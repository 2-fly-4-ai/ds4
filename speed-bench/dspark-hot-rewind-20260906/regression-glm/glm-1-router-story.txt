# The Keeper of Tomorrow

Elias Marsh had tended the light at Cape Disappointment for thirty-one years, and in all that time, the most exciting thing to arrive was a supply boat every second Thursday. So when he found the envelope wedged beneath the door of the lantern room—no boat had come, no footprints in the fresh snow—he stood a long while in the cold, simply looking at it.

The postmark read tomorrow's date.

He knew this the way you know your own name. He'd marked the calendar that morning, crossing off October the 14th with the same ritual he'd performed three decades running
