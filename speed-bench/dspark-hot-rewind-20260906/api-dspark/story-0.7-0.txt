The letter arrived on a Tuesday, a day that had begun with fog and ended with the same. Elias, the keeper of the Bent Lighthouse, found it in the brass mailbox that was bolted to the door, a box that had been empty for eleven years. The envelope was thick, the paper a heavy, cream-colored stock. The postmark was a smudge, but the date was unmistakable: October 14th, 1934. Tomorrow.

Elias’s hands, calloused from thirty years of turning the great lamp, trembled as he slit the flap. Inside was a single sheet of paper, folded with care. The handwriting was his own.

“Dear Elias,” it read. “You will read this on the 13th. I am writing to you from the 14th, because I know you will need to know what happens before it does. The storm will not hit the coast. It will hit the tower. The gale will be from the south-west, and it will break the third window on the gallery level. Do not try to fix it. Let the water in.
