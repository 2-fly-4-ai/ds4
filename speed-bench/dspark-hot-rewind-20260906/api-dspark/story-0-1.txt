The letter arrived on a Tuesday, carried in by the grey, churning sea. It wasn't in a bottle, but in a slick, waterproof pouch that had been tied to the leg of a bedraggled cormorant. The bird, exhausted, simply dropped it onto the lighthouse’s stone gallery and flew off into the mist.

Elias, the keeper, was a man of routine. He wound the clockwork, trimmed the wick, and recorded the weather in his logbook. He was suspicious of anything that broke the rhythm of the sea. But the pouch was sealed with a wax stamp bearing his own insignia—a compass rose with a cracked needle. He couldn't have forged it better himself.

Inside was a single sheet of thick, cream-colored paper. The handwriting was his own, though more hurried, the letters slanted as if written in a great haste.
