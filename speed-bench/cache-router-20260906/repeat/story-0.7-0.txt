The letter arrived on a Tuesday, a day that had begun with fog and ended with the same. Elias, the keeper of the Argus Light, found it in the brass tray where the supply boat left his mail. It was a single sheet of thick, cream-colored paper, sealed with a blob of red wax he didn't recognize. There was no stamp, no address, only his name in a neat, sloping hand he had never seen before.

He opened it with his blunt thumbnail. The paper felt cool and smooth. The message was short:

*Elias,*

*The fog will lift at 4:13 AM. Do not let the light go out. The storm will arrive by dusk. It will be the worst in twenty years. You must secure the eastern shutter. The one that sticks. Use the new brass hinge you ordered. It’s in the supply crate. I know you were going to use the old one, but it will fail. Trust me.*

*I am you. From tomorrow.*

Elias laughed, a dry, hollow sound that echoed in the stone room. He crumpled the letter and tossed it into the coal bin. A prank, he thought. Some fisherman’s son with too much time and a grudge
