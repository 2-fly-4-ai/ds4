The letter arrived on a Tuesday, carried in by the grey, churning sea. It wasn't in a bottle, but in a slick, waterproof pouch that had been tied to the leg of a bedraggled cormorant. The bird, exhausted, landed on the lighthouse railing, shook its feathers, and stared at Elias with a single, glassy eye before flying off.

Elias, a man of routine and solitude, was unnerved. He hadn't seen a bird in weeks, let alone one bearing mail. He unzipped the pouch with trembling fingers. The paper inside was thick, the ink a deep, steady blue. The date at the top made his breath catch.

*October 14th.*

But today was October 13th.

He read the letter, his heart a slow, heavy drum against his ribs.

*Dear Elias,*

*I am writing to you from tomorrow. I know this sounds impossible, but you must trust me. I am you. I am writing this at the same desk, in the same light, but the sea is different. It is calm. The wind is from the east. The storm you are expecting tonight will not come.*
