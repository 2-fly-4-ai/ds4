The letter arrived on a Tuesday, carried in by the grey, churning sea. It wasn't in a bottle, but in a slick, waterproof pouch that had been tied to the leg of a bedraggled gull. The gull, exhausted, simply dropped it on the lighthouse’s stone gallery and flew off, leaving a single feather as a receipt.

Elias, the keeper, was a man of routine. He wound the clockwork, trimmed the wick, and logged the weather. He was suspicious of anything that broke the rhythm of his solitary life. He picked up the pouch, feeling the weight of the paper inside. The return address was a single word: TOMORROW.

He opened it. The paper was thick, the ink a deep, steady blue. The handwriting was his own.

*Dear Elias,*

*I know you’re reading this on Tuesday, the 14th. You’re wondering who’s playing a trick. I’m not. I’m you. I’m writing this from Thursday, the 16th, and I’m sending it back to you because I need you to do something. It’s the only way to save the light.*
