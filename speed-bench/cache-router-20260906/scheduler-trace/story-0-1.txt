The letter arrived on a Tuesday, carried in by the grey, churning sea. Elias, the lighthouse keeper, had been expecting the supply boat, but this was no crate of salted pork and lamp oil. It was a single, water-stained envelope, sealed with a blob of red wax that looked disturbingly like dried blood.

He dried his hands on his oil-stained coat and broke the seal. The paper inside was thick, the handwriting a frantic, looping scrawl he didn't recognize.

*Elias,*

*I know you’re reading this on Tuesday. I’m writing it on Wednesday. I’m sorry. I can’t stop it. The light is going to fail at 2:14 AM. The gear in the main lamp will seize. You’ll be up in the tower, trying to fix it, and you’ll slip on the oil you spilled. You’ll hit your head. The *Annie Marlene* will be out there, and she won’t see the rocks. I’m so sorry.*

*Your friend,*
*Elias*
